The health check

Ten areas worth testing

These are the questions a full-time CTO would be asking every week. The health check works through each one and reports back in plain language.

  1. 1 Ownership & strategy

    Is technology ownership clear, and is there a practical plan aligned to growth, efficiency, customer and operating priorities?

  2. 2 Core systems & architecture

    Which platforms are business-critical, and which are becoming unreliable, unsupported, fragmented or a constraint on growth?

  3. 3 Suppliers & key people

    Could the environment be operated if a key provider or employee disappeared — and who independently represents your interests?

  4. 4 Cybersecurity & continuity

    Are backups recoverable, access controlled, MFA enabled and recovery responsibilities clear if systems fail or an incident occurs?

  5. 5 Technology spend

    Do you know the full annual cost across software, support, infrastructure, licences and projects — and whether it delivers value?

  6. 6 Data & decisions

    Can leaders get reliable answers quickly, or do spreadsheets, extracts and conflicting versions of the truth slow decisions?

  7. 7 Projects & priorities

    Who tests the business case, sequences dependencies, manages risk and checks that promised benefits are actually realised?

  8. 8 Connected experiences

    Do customer, employee and operational systems work together, or do gaps create duplicated data, manual work and frustration?

  9. 9 Automation & AI

    Where can simple integration and workflow automation remove repetitive effort before larger AI initiatives are considered?

  10. 10 Technology leadership

    Does the business have experienced, independent ownership of priorities, risk, suppliers and investment — even without a full-time CTO?
